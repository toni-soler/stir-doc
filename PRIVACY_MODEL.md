# Privacy model

Listings are authenticated and tenant-visible, not publicly indexed. Owner IDs are opaque; email, legal identity, credentials and financial risk are not listing data. Optional location is coarse text; do not enter private addresses or contact secrets. No uploads in 0.1 (see 0.4 below for real uploads).

Future messages and snapshots are party-visible. Legal identity is separate from ParticipantProfile. Marketplace reputation is independent from RiskSubject, Identity Assurance, RiskPolicy and Findings.

Snapshots stay in STIR. Hashing does not anonymize low-entropy data; use private random nonce, selective disclosure and a reviewed retention policy. Logs exclude tokens and request bodies. Moderation, retention and production privacy review remain gates.

0.3: only the AgreementSnapshot's SHA-256 digest crosses into the osTRIS journal (`contractualMetadataDigest`) - descriptions, messages, bios and every other STIR commercial/private field never do. Ed25519 private signing keys are generated client-side and never leave the browser: they are never sent to STIR, never sent to osTRIS, never custodied by either server. STIR only ever relays a signature the client itself produced.

0.4: Listing photos and profile avatars are real personal data once uploaded - authenticated, tenant-scoped, purpose-restricted (never publicly indexed, never accessible without a bearer token), and never trusted at the boundary (real magic-byte content sniffing, never the client's declared filename or Content-Type, so a client can never make its own upload masquerade as something else or escape its own tenant's object-storage prefix). A ContentReport's `reporterUserId` and free-text `reason` are visible only to accounts holding `stir.moderation.manage`, never to the reported party or any other tenant member. `ParticipantDeviceCredential.label` is a plain friendly name the user themselves chooses (e.g. "My laptop") - it is never osTRIS protocol data and carries no obligation of accuracy. STIR's own new log lines (rate-limit rejections, rejected uploads, moderation actions, device add/revoke) are structured, request-correlated (MDC), and never carry a JWT, a signature, a full signing payload, a private key, or a password - only ids and event codes. Local backup/restore (`scripts/backup.py`/`restore.py`) captures Postgres and object storage; a browser's own Ed25519 private signing key is explicitly and permanently NOT part of that backup - it never left the browser to begin with, so there is nothing server-side to lose or restore for it (a user who loses their only device loses that device's signing capability, recoverable only through the existing multi-device add/revoke flow on a still-active device, never through a server-side restore).
